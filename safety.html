Reference · Responsibility

Safety First

Every credible firearms organisation — military, police, and civilian — teaches the same short list of rules. They exist because nearly every recorded firearms accident traces back to one of them being skipped. If you remember nothing else from this site, remember these four.

01

Treat every weapon as loaded

Assume a firearm is loaded until you have personally verified otherwise — every time you pick it up, no exceptions. "I thought it was empty" is the opening line of most accident reports.

02

Never point at anything you don't intend to destroy

Control the muzzle direction at all times, even when unloaded. Point it at the ground or a safe direction. What the muzzle covers is what a mechanical failure could cost you.

03

Keep your finger off the trigger

Rest the finger straight along the frame outside the trigger guard until you are actually ready to fire. Startle, stumble, or bump — a finger off the trigger keeps them harmless.

04

Be sure of your target and beyond

Bullets don't stop at the thing you aimed at. Identify the target positively, and know what lies behind it — walls, roads, and people you never saw.

Between uses

Storage & transport

At home

Locked, unloaded, separate

Store firearms unloaded in a locked cabinet or safe, with ammunition locked away separately. Where children or unauthorised adults could reach them, use trigger or cable locks as well. Unloaded and locked also protects the weapon from theft.

In transit

Cased and out of sight

Carry firearms unloaded, in a locked case, with ammunition stored separately — the standard expected by airlines and most licensing regimes alike. Never display a firearm in public view, even legally owned, and never handle it in response to curiosity.

Maintenance

Clear it first, always

Before any cleaning or disassembly, unload and physically check the chamber. Most cleaning-bench accidents happen when someone assumed the round they had chambered earlier was gone. Wear eye protection; solvents and springs bite too.

Legal context · India

The law where our readers are

India — Arms Act, 1959 & Arms Rules, 2016

In India, no civilian may acquire, possess, or transport a firearm without a licence issued by the district or state licensing authority. Licences are granted only for specified purposes (such as target shooting or personal protection, subject to threat assessment) and carry strict storage, reporting, and renewal obligations. Airguns above prescribed energy limits are also regulated. If you are reading this from elsewhere: most jurisdictions have comparable licensing, registration, or permit systems — check your national and local law before anything else.
